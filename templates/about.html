How the predictions work

Data

Predictions come from the Cascade Gateway Border Data Warehouse, which records the measured delay in every lane every 5 minutes from road sensors, for Peace Arch and Pacific Highway in both directions. The models learn from data since January 2023, after the COVID border restrictions ended.

Model

Each lane has two models:

  • A typical-delay profile for each month, weekday and hour, adjusted for Canadian and US holidays and the days around them.
  • A gradient-boosted model (LightGBM) that starts from the profile and corrects it using the last few hours of delay and traffic volume, the same hour a day and a week earlier, and how far the day is from a holiday. It also uses the current delay in the other lanes and at the other crossing. It covers up to a week ahead; beyond that the page shows the typical delay.
  • A range from two more LightGBM models, for the 10th and 90th percentile, so the delay falls inside it about 8 times out of 10.
  • A 30-minute warning for car lanes, from a model of the chance that an hour averages over 30 minutes.
Accuracy

Tested on October 2025 to September 2026, a year the models never saw while being built, for southbound cars at Peace Arch the predicted hourly delay was off by 5 minutes on average one hour ahead and about 7 minutes a day or a week ahead. The 30-minute warning caught 85% of hours over 30 minutes one hour ahead and 78% a day ahead; 64% and 46% of warnings were right. Northbound and NEXUS delays are usually short and predicted within 1 to 3 minutes. Long northbound waits are rare and harder: the warning catches about half of them at Peace Arch and under a third at Pacific Highway, and most northbound warnings turn out shorter.

Delays are the time spent queueing as measured by the sensors, which can differ from the wait times CBP and CBSA post.
